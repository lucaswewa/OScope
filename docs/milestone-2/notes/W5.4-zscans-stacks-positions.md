## Summary

The patterns that go through focus, or visit a list of places, on the scan tab's plan ([spec](../phases.md#w5-4)):

- **A z-scan** at one place, and **a tiled z-stack** over a region: their range and step, with the count of planes, beside the data's size.
- **Positions:** places added from where the stage is, with its z, focused there by hand; each named, moved up or down, or removed; visited in turn. The pane says where the stage is, beside *Add where the stage is*.

W5.4 ends Stage W5 with **Checkpoint I**, below: an overview, a histo scan of the default slide, the mosaic watched, and the scan stitched. It is Milestone 1's Checkpoint D, in the app.

## What was built

In `web/src/features/scan/`:

- **`positions.ts`:** the list's editing, each place added with a name no other has, renamed, moved and removed; the places as the server's positions take them; and a stack's planes, as the server counts them.
- **`PlanPane.vue`:** the positions' list, a name field and *Move up*, *Move down* and *Remove* for each, and the stage's place beside *Add where the stage is*; and the plan's estimate, with its planes for a stack.
- **`plan.ts`, `planning.ts`:** the positions named, with their z.

The server already had what it needs: P35's planners, a position's name and its own z among them.

## How to try it

The try-its of W5 use the server built with CUDA, at the owner's choice: the simulated camera's frames on the GPU.

```sh
cargo build --release -p oscope-server --features cuda
npm --prefix web ci && npm --prefix web run build
target/release/oscope-server --config configs/oscope.toml --port 8211 --web web/dist
```

The spec's try-it is on the simulator's cell sample. Load it from a terminal:

```sh
curl -s -X POST http://127.0.0.1:8211/api/v1/simulation/load -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"name": "fluorescent cells"}'
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8211/#/control` and take control. Press *Home*, *Move to* the middle of the slide's cells, press *Start*, and *Focus*. Open `#/scan`, on *Plan a scan*:

1. **A z-stack:** choose *Tiled z-stack*, and type a region of some 0.3 × 0.2 mm about the stage, through the camera's small region. Type *Below* 10, *Above* 10 and *Step* 1: the estimate says 21 planes, beside the tiles and the data. Run it into a project: each tile's 21 planes, from the lowest.
2. **Positions:** choose *Positions*. Jog to a cell, and press *Add where the stage is*: the line beside the button says where the stage is, and the list gains *Position 1*, at the stage's x, y and z. Jog to two more, and add each. Rename one; move the last up, first. Run them: they are visited in the list's order, each at its own z.

## Checkpoint I

A slide scanned from the app: its overview, its tissue, a histo scan of it, the mosaic as it fills, and the scan stitched, on the simulator's default slide. It is Milestone 1's Checkpoint D, in the app.

### The demo

On the CUDA build, as above.

1. **Get ready:** open `http://127.0.0.1:8211/#/control`, take control, and press *Home*. *Move to* x 40, y 12 and z 5 mm, over the default slide's tissue; press *Start*, and *Focus*. Fit the 5× under *The objective*.
2. **The overview:** open `#/scan`, its tab *Histo scan*. Type the region x 35, y 8.5, width 10.5 and height 7 mm, and press *Make the overview*: its progress, then the slide, in about a minute.
3. **The tissue:** *Next*. Its islands, its area and its threshold are said.
4. **The regions:** *Next*. A region is outlined about each island. Untick *Keep it* on the small one. Press *Draw it again* on the large one, and drag a rectangle of some 2 × 1.5 mm inside it.
5. **The focus map:** *Next*. Choose 2 columns and 2 rows. On the Control tab, fit the 10×; back on the scan tab, the map's points show on the view.
6. **The run:** *Next*. Choose a project, or make one, and press *Run the scan*. The run measures the map first, then takes the tiles: watch the live mosaic fill in under the plan, each tile turning green as it is taken, the next marked. *Pause*, then *Resume*. When it ends, *The focus map* says its surface and each point's focus.
7. **The stitch:** press *Stitch it*: the stitch job's state, then *Open the stitched image*, which W6 fills.

### The checklist

- [ ] The overview is made of the region typed, and shown on the view.
- [ ] The tissue step says the islands found, their area and the threshold.
- [ ] A region is proposed about each island; one dropped is drawn fainter and left out; one drawn again is what the plan covers.
- [ ] The focus map's points show where the run will measure.
- [ ] The run measures the map, then takes the tiles; the mosaic fills in under the plan, each tile marked as it is taken.
- [ ] *Pause* holds the stage; *Resume* goes on from the next tile.
- [ ] At its end, the map is said: its surface, how well it fits, each point's focus.
- [ ] *Stitch it* queues the stitch job, and says its state and its result.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **Positions are added with the stage's z,** where its focus was set by hand, as the server's positions take it: each is visited at its own z, whatever the run's focus.
- **The stage's place is said beside *Add where the stage is*:** what the button adds, before it is pressed.
- **A position is named as no other is,** *Position 4* if *Position 3* is taken; renamed, its name goes to the server, a blank one left out.
- **Reordering is by *Move up* and *Move down*,** a button each, which the keyboard reaches; not by dragging.
- **The planes are counted in the app,** as the server counts them, from the plan's stack: the estimate's frames are planes times tiles, and its data their size.
- **W5.1 made most of the patterns' forms:** the z-scan's place and stack, the tiled stack's region, and the positions added. W5.4 adds the names, the order and the planes.

## Follow-ups and known gaps

- **The positions are reordered by buttons,** not by dragging, as W4.1's queue is: the buttons are enough for a short list.
- **A position's z is the stage's when added:** it can't be typed, nor set again without removing and adding it.
- **From W5.1 to W5.3, found and not fixed:** P34's autofocus from the top of the travel sweeps past it and fails; a measured map's autofocus runs aren't in the plan's estimate; and the estimate's time is the real camera's, quicker than the simulator.
